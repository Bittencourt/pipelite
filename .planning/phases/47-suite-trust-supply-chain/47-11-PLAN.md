<objective>
Put the audit policy on the check that actually blocks a merge, and keep the workflow's own gates honest
in the same commit.

Purpose: 47-CONTEXT is explicit — *"The gate belongs on the required CI job, consistent with Phase 46's
Route A reasoning: a non-blocking gate is a vacuous gate."* A new workflow file publishes a check-run
name that is absent from the master ruleset and can go red for weeks without blocking a single merge.
So the step lands on the existing job `ci`, whose name IS the required check-run name.

Two steps are added: the policy gate (`npm run security:audit`) and `npm audit signatures`, which
detects tarball tampering that advisory data cannot see at all — measured today at **1104/1104 verified
registry signatures and 169 verified attestations, zero failures, in 102 s**. 47-CONTEXT decided to
accept that minute: taking the required check from ~95 s to ~200 s to keep a tamper-detection gate is a
good trade, and the job's `timeout-minutes: 15` has ample room. The cost goes in the step's comment so
the next person weighing it has the number.

Four assertions in `db-test-isolation.test.ts` constrain `ci.yml` and must not be tripped, and one
constant in that same file — `MIN_CI_STEPS`, with its measured enumeration in a doc-comment — goes
STALE the moment a step is added. That comment is updated in the same commit as the workflow change,
because a floor and its prose in disagreement is the exact shape Phase 46 was burned by.

Output: two CI steps on the required job, and the workflow's own gates re-anchored in one commit.
